Glossary & Help

Glossary & Help

Modes

TX (Transmit): Practice sending Morse code using the paddle or keyboard.

RX (Receive): Practice listening to Morse code and typing what you hear.

QSO (Conversation): Simulate real-world radio contacts with simulated interference and fading.

WPM (Words Per Minute)

The speed at which Morse code is sent or received. Higher WPM means faster characters and shorter spaces.

Farnsworth Timing

A method for learning Morse code where individual characters are sent at a fast speed (e.g., 20 WPM), but the spaces between characters and words are stretched out to lower the overall speed (e.g., 10 WPM). This helps you learn the "sound" of the character without having to think as fast.

Koch Method

A learning method where you start with just two characters at full speed. Once you achieve 90% accuracy, you add another character. This prevents you from building a "lookup table" in your head and trains your reflexes directly.

QRM & QSB

QRM (Interference): Man-made interference from other stations or electronics. Adds static and noise to the signal.

QSB (Fading): Natural variations in signal strength due to atmospheric conditions. The signal will fade in and out.

Hide QSO Text

Hides the incoming and outgoing text during a QSO, forcing you to rely entirely on your ears to copy the message.

Keyer Options

Straight Key: A simple up/down switch. You manually control the length of every dot and dash.

Iambic A: A dual-lever paddle. Holding one side sends continuous dots, the other sends dashes. Squeezing both sends alternating dots and dashes.

Iambic B: Similar to A, but if you release the squeeze, it will complete the opposite element before stopping. Preferred by many fast operators.

Advanced Timing

Strict Timing: Enforces perfect spacing between elements. If disabled, the keyer is more forgiving.

Weighting: The ratio of dash length to dot length. Standard is 3.0 (a dash is 3 times longer than a dot).

Word Space: The gap between words, measured in dot lengths. Standard is 7.
